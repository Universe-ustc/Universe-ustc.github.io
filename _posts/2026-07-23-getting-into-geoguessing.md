I have always been curious about world geography — especially **cultures**: how people live, what cities feel like, what everyday details look like in different countries. Whenever TikTok serves a video about a place's culture or daily life, I usually watch it to the end. On Bilibili I occasionally ran into clips about **geo-guessing** (图寻) — the game of looking at a street-view scene and figuring out where you are. I liked the idea for a long time before I finally tried it myself.

Once I did, I realized it is not a casual party trick. It is a whole learning system: vegetation, alphabet, road markings, utility poles, driving side, architecture — and a lot of patient practice.

---

## What Is Geo-Guessing?

In short: you are dropped into a photo or street-view location (sometimes a walking video), and you must place a pin on the map — ideally as close as possible to the true spot.

Beginners often start with broad questions:

- Which **continent** does this look like?
- What **language** appears on signs?
- Is traffic on the **left or right**?
- Do the plants, soil, or sky feel tropical, arid, or temperate?

Experienced players go much further — specific ferns, bollards, license-plate shapes, camera coverage patterns. For me, the appeal is that it turns geography into a detective game.

---

## Starting with GeoGuessr

I began on [GeoGuessr's onboarding game](https://www.geoguessr.com/onboarding/game). The interface is polished: compass, timer, mini-map, and a clean Street View feel.

![GeoGuessr gameplay with street view and mini-map](/assets/img/posts/geoguessr-gameplay.png)

The catch for free users is the daily limit — about **five rounds** a day. That is enough to taste the hobby, not enough to train seriously.

My early accuracy was... educational. In one round I landed in a fern-heavy forested road and guessed somewhere in North America. The true location was **New Zealand**, more than 13,000 km away — basically 1 point out of 5,000.

![GeoGuessr result: guessed North America, actual location New Zealand](/assets/img/posts/geoguessr-result-nz.png)

The post-round "clues" were only mildly useful. They tend to be very broad (for example, "ferns are common in New Zealand forests"), so they confirm the answer more than they teach you how to spot the next one.

## Looking for Free Alternatives

After hitting the free-round wall, I searched for sites that are free or almost free. Three stood out — with very different trade-offs.

### OpenGuessr

[OpenGuessr](https://openguessr.com/) lets you play world locations without the same hard daily wall. It works. The UI is simpler: fewer competitive features, and in my sessions I did not get the same sense of timer pressure or polished multiplayer that GeoGuessr offers.

![OpenGuessr coastal street-view gameplay](/assets/img/posts/openguessr-gameplay.png)

Good for casual practice. Less good if you want a full "game product" experience.

### WorldGuessr

[WorldGuessr](https://www.worldguessr.com/) is also free and easy to open. In the mode I tried, though, the challenge often reduces to **picking a country flag** from a short list. That is useful for absolute beginners learning continents and countries, but it feels narrow once you want real pin-on-map skill.

![WorldGuessr country-flag guessing mode](/assets/img/posts/worldguessr-flags.png)

### City Guesser (strongly recommended)

Among the free options, I like [City Guesser](https://cityguesser.eu/) the most.

Instead of only static panoramas, you watch **first-person videos filmed while walking through a city** — streets, shops, stations, parks, whatever the clip happens to show. That feels closer to "being there."

You can also **choose a geographic circle** before playing: set a center and radius, see how many videos are available, then confirm.

![City Guesser settings: choose a play circle on the world map](/assets/img/posts/cityguesser-settings.png)

![City Guesser: first-person video walking through a city](/assets/img/posts/cityguesser-gameplay.png)

After each guess you get a clear distance and score on the map — useful feedback for learning.

![City Guesser result map showing distance from the true location](/assets/img/posts/cityguesser-result.png)

If you want free practice with richer visual context, start here.

## A Hidden Gem: Tuxun (图寻)

One frustration with many overseas sites is the lack of **explanations**. You miss, you shrug, you move on.

Later I found a Chinese site mentioned on a WeChat public account also called 图寻: [tuxun.fun](https://tuxun.fun/).

It is denser than a single game page. There are daily challenges, matchmaking, parties, question banks, even OSINT-style modes — and importantly, **lots of solutions / explanations**.

![Tuxun home with multiple game modes](/assets/img/posts/tuxun-home.png)

What impressed me most is the **tutorial library**: China guides, world guides (including community-translated material such as Plonk It by continent), techniques, and geography notes. It feels like a curriculum, not only a lobby.

![Tuxun documentation sidebar with world tutorials](/assets/img/posts/tuxun-tutorials.png)

For structured explanations after each miss — plus a large bank of China and world questions — this was the "treasure" I had been looking for.

---

## Where I Am Now

On global rounds I still cannot reliably pick the correct **country**, let alone the city block. That is fine. The hobby rewards slow accumulation: one alphabet today, one plant cue tomorrow, one road-marking pattern next week.

My current stack is roughly:

| Goal | Site |
|------|------|
| Taste the classic experience | [GeoGuessr](https://www.geoguessr.com/onboarding/game) |
| Free city-video practice | [City Guesser](https://cityguesser.eu/) |
| Structured learning + explanations | [Tuxun](https://tuxun.fun/) |
| Quick free rounds | [OpenGuessr](https://openguessr.com/) / [WorldGuessr](https://www.worldguessr.com/) |

Geo-guessing turned a vague interest in world geography into something I can practice every day. I am still at the beginning, and the road is long and hard — but that is also why it is worth continuing.
